## Source-grounded construct

**SOURCE:** AERA standards address responsible conduct of education research. AERA / APA / NCME standards address validity, reliability / precision, fairness, accessibility, interpretation, responsibility, and consequences in testing. APA JARS specifies transparent reporting practices. QAA Education Studies provides a sector-led field context for critical inquiry, evidence, educational systems, policy, practice, and real-world application.

## Competency boundary

**INFERENCE:** The textbook competency is to design, conduct, interpret, report, critique, and communicate education research with transparent evidence, responsible methods, appropriate measurement, and attention to consequences.  
**NOT CLAIMED:** Reporting compliance alone proves valid research; the QAA benchmark is not a universal PhD research standard; testing standards apply when measurement is in scope.

## Construct decomposition

1. Frame a researchable question and contribution.
2. Select design and methods consistent with the question and context.
3. Define constructs, populations, measures, data, and limitations.
4. Analyze evidence transparently and distinguish results from interpretation.
5. Examine validity, uncertainty, fairness, accessibility, and consequences.
6. Report methods, deviations, analyses, and claims for scrutiny and replication.
7. Communicate to scholarly, practitioner, participant, and public audiences.

## Evidence and task model

**CROSSWALK:** Research protocol, preregistration, construct / measure map, analysis plan, data or measure audit, manuscript reporting checklist, limitations memo, peer-review response, and replication / sensitivity plan.  
**EVALUATOR DESIGN:** Evaluate claim-to-evidence alignment, design fit, transparency, uncertainty, ethical / equity reasoning, reporting completeness, and interpretation boundaries.

## Textbook spine

`Question → Design → Define and measure → Analyze → Interpret → Report → Review → Replicate / improve`

## Failure modes and constraints

- question-method mismatch;
- construct underdefinition;
- p-value or model output treated as the claim;
- omitted missingness or subgroup limitations;
- preregistration / exploratory distinction erased;
- reporting checklist used as a proxy for validity;
- student / participant privacy breach;
- agent-generated literature synthesis accepted without source checking.
